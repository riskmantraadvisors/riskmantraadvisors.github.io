Supervisory inspections have shifted decisively from document review to data interrogation.
Examiners arrive with analytics of their own and test whether an institution's reported numbers,
limits and policies are consistent with what its systems actually show.

## The new failure modes

- **Policy–practice gaps.** A limit defined in policy that is not configured — or not enforced — in
  the system.
- **Reconciliation breaks.** Exposures that do not tie between the risk system, the general ledger
  and regulatory returns.
- **Unexplained overrides.** Manual adjustments without an audit trail or approval.
- **Stale assumptions.** Behavioural or model assumptions not refreshed since they were approved.

## Running a mock inspection

A mock inspection is most valuable when it mirrors how supervisors actually work:

1. Request data extracts, not presentations.
2. Re-perform a sample of key calculations independently.
3. Trace every material limit from policy to system to report.
4. Interview first-line owners, not only the risk function.
5. Grade findings by severity and agree time-bound remediation.

## The Board's role

Boards should ask a simple question each quarter: *if the supervisor arrived tomorrow, which three
findings would we expect?* If management cannot answer, the readiness work has not started.
