When institutions think about model risk, they often think first of the annual validation report.
Validation matters — but a model can pass validation and still create risk if it is used outside
its design, fed with poor data or changed without control.

## The model life cycle

An effective Model Risk Management (MRM) framework covers each stage:

1. **Inventory and tiering** — a complete register of models, owners and uses, with materiality
   tiers that drive the depth of review.
2. **Development standards** — documented methodology, data lineage and testing before approval.
3. **Independent validation** — conceptual soundness, data checks, benchmarking, back-testing and
   sensitivity analysis.
4. **Implementation control** — confirming that the model running in production is the model that
   was validated.
5. **Ongoing monitoring** — performance thresholds and triggers for re-validation.
6. **Change management** — controlled, documented changes with impact assessment.

## Where frameworks break

The most common weaknesses are not in the mathematics. They are an incomplete inventory, spreadsheet
"models" that sit outside governance, and production implementations that drift from the validated
version. Each is a governance failure that a validation exercise alone will not detect.

## A pragmatic first step

Start with the inventory. Ask every function which calculations drive a decision, a limit or a
reported number. The answer is almost always longer than the existing register.
