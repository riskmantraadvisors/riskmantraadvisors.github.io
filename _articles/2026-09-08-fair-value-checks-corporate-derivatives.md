For many mid-sized corporates, derivatives are bought rather than built. The bank structures the
product, quotes the price and provides the month-end mark-to-market. Without an independent view,
the corporate has no easy way to know whether it paid a fair spread — or whether the product even
matched its hedging need.

## What an independent fair-value check covers

- **Entry pricing.** Re-pricing the trade at inception using observable market data to estimate the
  embedded margin.
- **Structure suitability.** Confirming the payoff actually offsets the underlying exposure, rather
  than introducing a speculative leg.
- **Ongoing marks.** Independently validating bank-provided MTMs used in financial statements and
  hedge-effectiveness testing.
- **Documentation.** Ensuring the hedge relationship, objective and effectiveness method are
  documented in line with the applicable accounting framework.

## Common findings

Across engagements, the recurring themes are consistent: spreads well above what the risk
justifies, structures more complex than the exposure requires, and hedge documentation that would
struggle under audit. None of these are visible without an independent model.

## Getting started

A one-time review of the existing book usually identifies whether a deeper engagement is
warranted. From there, a lightweight *virtual risk office* — periodic valuation, pricing support
before new trades, and an annual policy review — gives senior treasury oversight without adding
headcount.
